Dialogue Between Civilizations

An atomic-scale sculpture inspired by the Great Pyramid of Giza.
Abstract

Dialogue Between Civilizations is a replica of the Great Pyramid of Giza rebuilt at the nanoscale, in gold. Its proportions follow those of the pyramid — the relationships with π, φ and the 51.8° slope — carried down to a structure invisible to the naked eye.

Gold is not incidental. The original pyramids are believed to have been crowned with gold capstones — emblems of divinity and power — so here the entire monument is rebuilt in gold. Bridging Ancient Egypt and contemporary nanotechnology, the work becomes a reflection on scale, on cultural memory and on what endures as the measure of a monument shifts from the colossal to the imperceptible.

The two images below hold the same form at opposite ends of scale. On the left, the Great Pyramid of Giza — millions of tonnes of stone raised by hand, its proportions tuned to π, φ and the 51.8° slope. On the right, gold nanocrystals a few tens of nanometres across which, left to self-assemble, spontaneously organise into pyramids of that very same geometry, invisible to the naked eye. This is the dialogue at the heart of the project: a single shape that recurs across civilisations and across scales — from the monumental to the atomic.

02 — Production

A pyramid that grows itself

For production at a larger nanoscale, the pyramid is not carved but grown. Drawing on advances in the self-assembly of polyhedral nanocrystals, shape-controlled gold nanocrystals — octahedra and corner-truncated octahedra, tens of nanometres across — are suspended with a surfactant (CTAC) and left to organise as a droplet of solution slowly evaporates on a substrate. Because the particles' own shape dictates the geometry of the resulting supercrystal, these gold polyhedra settle layer upon layer into square-pyramidal supercrystals: a monument that assembles itself from the bottom up. A complementary surfactant-diffusion route can grow free-standing supercrystals directly in solution.

Production method after M. H. Huang & S. Thoka, “Formation of supercrystals through self-assembly of polyhedral nanocrystals,” Nano Today 10 (2015) 81–92.

03 — The relations

Where geometry meets the cosmos

  • IThe ratio between the perimeter and the height reflects a design based on the relationship between the triangle, the circle and the square — which in turn are the sphere, the cube and the pyramid.
    P / h = 921.6 / 146.6 ≈ 6.28 = 2π
  • IIThe ratio between the height (h) and the semi-base (a) approaches the golden ratio: the height is roughly 1.618 times the semi-base.
    h / a ≈ 1.618 = φ
  • IIIMultiplying the pyramid's original height (146.6 m) by 43,200 approximates the Earth's polar radius; multiplying the base perimeter by the same factor gives the Earth's circumference — suggesting an advanced knowledge of the planet's form and dimensions.
  • IVThe 51.84° face angle carries several implications. tan(51.84°) ≈ 4/3 — the ratio between the height and the semi-base, a simple and elegant proportion. The angle sits close to key divisions in sacred geometry: the regular pentagon (linked to φ), the Kepler triangle (which combines π and φ), and the angles used in constructions related to the Platonic solids such as the tetrahedron and the icosahedron. Some researchers suggest the pyramid's angles could reflect cosmic relationships, such as the angle between the plane of Earth's orbit and the galactic plane.
    tan(θ) = h / a ≈ 51.8°
