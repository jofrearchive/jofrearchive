Biomimetic Solar

An artistic and technological investigation into solar panels made through biomimetic design, organic material experimentation and community-oriented DIY production.

Project Abstract

The project rethinks the solar panel as a functional device, sculptural object and pedagogical tool. Inspired by plant morphology and ecosophy, it explores solar technologies at mural and architectural scale through the use of electrochemical coatings applied onto lightweight substrates. The research focuses on screen printing as a reproducible fabrication method, allowing photovoltaic structures to be produced through accessible, low-cost processes linked to DIY culture and collective experimentation. In this way, light-harvesting systems can become more accessible, culturally embedded and ecologically regenerative.

01 / Conceptual Framework

From solar technology to cultural artifact

The project starts from a critical view of the green transition when it reproduces technocratic and extractivist logics. Rather than treating the solar panel as a neutral industrial unit, this research proposes it as a symbolic, aesthetic and civic interface. Form is not secondary to function: morphology becomes a way to rethink how energy is captured, understood and shared.

Biomimicry provides the formal intelligence of the project, not as decorative imitation but as a study of how vegetal systems optimise light, distribute matter and adapt to environmental conditions. Ecosophy grounds its ethical position, insisting that environmental questions are inseparable from social relations and subjective imaginaries. The project opens a dialogue between plant structures, mineral processes and technical fabrication.

02 / Project Principles

Biomimetic design

Study leaf venation, spiral arrangements, radial organisations and adaptive geometries that maximise light exposure and structural efficiency.

Material experimentation

Test translucent cellulose, conductive inks, copper plates, oxides, organic pigments and biodegradable supports in a low-tech research environment.

Distributed pedagogy

Turn each prototype into a transmissible tool: something that can be shown, narrated, replicated and shared through workshops and open documentation.

Solar justice

Shift renewable energy from a centralised commodity toward a collectively owned practice rooted in accessibility, autonomy and local making.

03 / Work Plan

Research to installation

Phase 1
Research & Design

Study plant morphologies, assess translucent photovoltaic materials and produce initial sketches and digital prototypes.

Phase 2
Prototype Development

Screen print first modules, test different leaf-based patterns and evaluate basic efficiency under varying light conditions.

Phase 3
Installation & Testing

Design an immersive display, refine positioning for natural and artificial light, and document all adjustments.

Phase 4
Exhibition & Dissemination

Present prototypes publicly through exhibition, workshops and online educational material for open replication.

Methodological Structure

The methodology is transdisciplinary. It combines practice-based artistic research, empirical observation of plant systems, digital modelling and experimental fabrication. The goal is not to copy nature literally, but to extract structural logics from it.

Observation begins with botanical references, field photography, close analysis and three-dimensional capture. These are translated into parametric and sculptural models that privilege surface orientation, modularity and reproducibility.

Prototyping focuses on accessible processes: handmade photolithography, electrolysis, screen printing and low-voltage circuits. Each prototype is treated at once as energy device, object of contemplation and critical statement about how technology could be organised differently.

Evaluation includes technical performance, environmental behaviour, structural stability and communicative clarity: can the piece transmit another relation between energy, matter, aesthetics and collective life?

Project Position

The solar panel becomes not only a machine for electricity, but a membrane for knowledge, a sculptural interface and a shared tool for ecological imagination.

04 / Cultural & Social Impact

A regenerative model of artistic technology

Artistic Innovation

Recasts renewable energy infrastructure as a site for visual culture, material experimentation and public engagement.

Community Inclusion

Uses workshops, collaborative making and accessible formats to involve low-income and disabled communities in solar knowledge production.

Environmental Responsibility

Prioritises local and biodegradable material directions, modular construction and a circular approach to fabrication and maintenance.

Beyond exhibition value, the project operates as a long-term platform for education, replication and local empowerment. It proposes a future in which clean energy is not only consumed but culturally produced: made by communities, shaped by ecological intelligence and embedded in everyday life.
